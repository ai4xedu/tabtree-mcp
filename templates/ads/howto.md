## The outline

This is the exact outline TabTree builds. Indentation is the hierarchy, and
`[ ]` marks a checkbox. You can paste it straight into TabTree, or into any
outliner that understands indentation.

```text
🎓 How to … in 5 steps
  > Replace this line with the promise. “How to X in 5 steps” is a headline; “My guide” is not.
  🪝 Hook @a:target
    > Say what they will be able to do, and for whom. If this line is vague, nothing after it lands.
    🎯 The promise — what they can do tonight and could not this morning
    👤 Written for ONE person. Name them, never “everyone”
    ⏱️ 20 min · 🟢 Nothing to install
    🧰 Have this open before step 1
  1️⃣ Set the stage @a:laptop
    > Every guide loses people at setup. Do it with them, slowly, and say what the screen should look like.
    👉 The boring step everyone skips — do it with them
    ✅ What they should see when it worked
    ⚠️ Where nine out of ten get stuck, and why
  2️⃣ Get the first win @a:star
    > Give them something visible in under two minutes. This is the moment they decide to finish or leave.
    👉 The smallest thing that already works
    ✅ Something visible, in under two minutes
    💡 This is where they decide to keep reading
  3️⃣ Do the real thing @a:rocket
    > The core move. One idea done properly beats five explained in passing.
    👉 The move the whole guide exists for
    ✅ What “done” looks like, precisely
    ⚠️ The shortcut that looks faster and is not
  4️⃣ Make it yours @a:idea
    > Hand it over: the one setting that makes the result theirs, and how to choose between the options.
    👉 The one setting worth changing
    ✅ Same result, in their own voice
    💡 Three options, and when each one is right
  5️⃣ Ship it @a:flag
    > A guide that ends without shipping is a bookmark. Send them out with something in their hands.
    👉 Put it in front of one real person today
    ✅ The proof — a link, a file, a screenshot
    🎁 What to do with it next
  🏁 Payoff @a:trophy
    > Close on the result, not on a summary — then ask for the one thing you actually want back.
    🏆 What they have now — a result, not a task
    🚫 The mistake almost everyone makes
    ➡️ The next thing worth learning
    💬 The question to ask in the caption
```
